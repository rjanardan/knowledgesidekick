A business and technical guide

Agentic AI systems fail less from weak models than from weak knowledge.

When an AI agent plans, calls tools, remembers, or grounds its answers, the limiting factor is rarely the model alone. It is what the system knows, how that knowledge is represented, how current it is, and how much of it it is allowed to see. This site is a guide for leaders funding or building agentic AI who want to treat knowledge as a first-class layer rather than an afterthought.

Written by Janardan Revuru · Get in touch · Portfolio: janalogy.com

The problem, stated plainly

Most agentic AI projects do not stumble because the underlying model cannot reason. They stumble because the system is asked to act on knowledge it does not reliably have.

An agent that can write a plausible plan but cannot tell which procedure is current, which customer record is authoritative, or which fact it is allowed to use will produce confident mistakes. In enterprise settings those mistakes are operational. In personal settings they are private. Either way, the cost is usually attributed to the model when it is actually a knowledge problem.

This matters more now than it did for chatbot-style systems, because agents act. A system that only answers can be wrong in a low-stakes way. A system that retrieves a number, runs an API call, changes a record, or summarizes a private inbox on the basis of stale or missing knowledge can do real damage at scale and at speed.

Who this is for

This site is written for the person who signs off on agentic AI work and the person who has to make it behave. Concretely:

  • A leader or manager deciding whether a knowledge layer is worth treating as a first-class investment.
  • An architect or engineer who is being asked to make an agent reliable, cheap enough to run, and safe enough to use on real data.
  • A team that has a model working in a demo and is now discovering that the real work is knowing what to believe, what is current, and what the system is allowed to see.

If you already have a working demo and are now discovering that the interesting questions are about knowledge, freshness, context, and grounding, you are exactly the audience.
