What knowledge audits and benchmarks are

An audit is an honest read on the state of your knowledge layer. Benchmarks tell you how that state compares to reasonable expectations for the system you are building.

Audits and benchmarks are the practical counterpart to the maturity evaluation. The maturity evaluation establishes where you stand on the lifecycle dimensions; audits and benchmarks go deeper on the specific knowledge that matters for the systems you are building — what is current, what is stale, what is missing, what is unowned, and what is safe to use.

They are useful because knowledge problems are easy to misread. A system that seems to work can be acting on stale knowledge, missing context, or unowned knowledge. An audit makes that visible before it becomes a production failure — or before a production failure gets misattributed to the model.

What an audit covers

The audit is scoped to the knowledge that matters for the systems you are building. At a high level it covers:

  • Current vs stale. What knowledge is current, what is stale, and how fast it is drifting from reality.
  • Missing knowledge. What is missing — the gaps that matter for the systems you are building, and where the useful knowledge actually lives.
  • Ownership. What is owned, what is unowned, and where the accountability behind the knowledge has weakened with turnover or handoff.
  • Safety to use. What is safe to use, what is not, and where the knowledge layer is exposing the systems to confident mistakes.
  • Benchmarks. How your knowledge stands against reasonable expectations for the kind of system you are building — enterprise agents, LLM products, or personal assistants.

The audit is scoped to what matters, not to an abstract ideal. It is practical, not academic.

When audits and benchmarks are most useful

Audits and benchmarks are useful at any point, but they are especially useful in production — when the problems are already showing up and you need an honest, independent read on where the knowledge layer stands and what to fix first.

They are also useful at inception, when you want to know where you stand before you build — not to judge, but to make the lifecycle, formats, training, and AEO work target the gaps that actually matter.
